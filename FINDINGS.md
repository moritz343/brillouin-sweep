# Findings (technical note, v0.1.0, 2026-11-05)

Moritz Wenzel

This note summarises what the code and data in this archive show so far. It is a
working record, not a peer-reviewed result. Numbers come from the scripts listed
next to each claim and can be reproduced with them.

## Question

A band diagram samples the Floquet–Bloch dispersion only on the edges of the
irreducible Brillouin zone (IBZ). For a 3D unit cell, when does that miss the
true edges of a complete band gap? Do the IBZ faces (sampled as a 2D "net",
shown in `web/brillouin-surface.html`) recover the error, or is a volume search
needed? This is the 3D counterpart of Maurin, Claeys, Deckers & Desmet,
*Int. J. Solids Struct.* 135 (2018), who studied it for 2D plane groups.

## 1. Mass-in-mass spring lattice (analytic model)

Host masses on a simple cubic lattice with axial springs k_x, k_y, k_z (shear
ratio s), diagonal springs g, and a resonator on springs r_x, r_y, r_z inside
each host. Gap between bands 3 and 4.

* **No diagonal springs (g = 0): the path is exact.** Each matrix entry is
  linear in each of cos k_x, cos k_y, cos k_z. The directions of motion are
  independent, each band is monotonic in one such function, and these functions
  have their extremes at the corners of the box.
* **Mirror-symmetric diagonal pairs** ((1,1,0) with (1,−1,0) etc.) keep that
  structure in the diagonal entries (cos(k_x+k_y) + cos(k_x−k_y) = 2 cos k_x cos k_y).
  In 206 random cells both gap edges were at corners (`study/` and the page).
  Exception, found later: for nearly equal springs, s ≈ 0.55, g ≈ k/2, the three
  polarisations mix along the body diagonal and the top of band 3 moves into the
  volume. Example k = (1, 0.9, 0.8), r = 2, μ = 0.2, s = 0.55, g = 0.5: maximum at
  (0.694, 0.660, 0.614) π/a; path overestimates the gap by 4.9 %, faces by 4.5 %.
* **One diagonal without its mirror partner** (only (1,1,0); Laue class 2/m,
  IBZ k_x ∈ [−π, π]): along k_x = k_y the (1,1,0)-polarised mode is exact with
  stiffness 2(1+s)k(1 − cos t) + 2g(1 − cos 2t), which has a maximum inside the
  face once **g > (1+s)k/4**; the rise above the edges is largest near
  g ≈ (1+s)k/2. Example k = (0.8, 1.3, 0.6), r = (10, 2.5, 8), μ = 0.6, s = 0.6,
  g = 1.6: path gap 0.509, true gap 0.437 (+16 %); with k = (0.3, 1.7, 0.1),
  r = (25, 0.7, 20), μ = 2, s = 0.15, g = 0.8: +48 % (`study/symmetry_pair.py`).
* **Size of the effect** depends on the resonators: with resonator springs
  softer than the host the resonance flattens the bands below the gap and the
  difference drops to about 0.1 % of the frequency.

## 2. Where band maxima sit, by symmetry

Symmetry facts: time reversal makes the 8 corner points (k ≡ −k) critical for
every band; on a mirror plane the normal derivative vanishes; on a face that is
not a mirror plane nothing vanishes by symmetry.

| Cell | Laue class | IBZ faces that are mirror planes | Max. on a non-mirror face | Max. inside the volume | Faces wrong by > 1 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all six ⟨110⟩ pairs | mmm | all 6 | – | narrow window only | 0 of 206 |
| only (1,1,0) | 2/m | 2 of 6 | 0 of 311 | 0 of 311 | 0 of 311 |
| only (1,1,0), (0,1,1), (1,0,1) | −1 | none | – | 108 of 195 | 47 of 195 (up to 92 %) |

Scripts: `study/monoclinic_faces.py`, `study/triclinic_test.py`.

**Rule of thumb (primitive lattices):** the faces suffice when every IBZ face is
a mirror plane, i.e. for the Laue classes generated by mirrors (mmm, 4/mmm,
6/mmm, m−3m); otherwise sample the mirror faces plus the volume. This is
evidence from one model family, not a theorem: symmetry never forbids a
critical point in general position (see the mmm exception above). For centred
lattices parts of the zone boundary are not mirror planes and need checking.

## 3. FEM: Liu-type locally resonant cell

`cells/liu_cubic.json`: lead sphere r = 5 mm, 2.5 mm silicone coating, epoxy,
simple cubic a = 15.5 mm (after Liu et al., *Science* 289, 2000), solved with
`voxel_fem.py` at 16³ voxels, 12 bands.

* 84-point grid over the IBZ tetrahedron (`data/liu/`): complete gap
  459.6–759.3 Hz (49 % of mid-gap); path, faces and volume agree. Flat bands at
  344.5 Hz (triply degenerate) and 1371–1383 Hz.
* 145-point polar sweep along the three faces through Γ (`data/liu_polar/`,
  `polar_kpoints.py`): the band above the gap rises from 759 Hz at Γ to about
  1.37 kHz within 6 % of Γ–X; near-Γ longitudinal phase velocity 652 m/s along
  [100] vs 448 m/s along [111] (at 190–390 Hz, not static).
* **Caveat:** one voxel (0.97 mm) is wider than the 0.5 mm epoxy gap between
  neighbouring coatings, so in the model the silicone shells touch along the
  cube axes. Convergence at 20³–24³ has not been checked.
